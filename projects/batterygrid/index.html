A few more details.

Is BatteryGrid free?

BatteryGrid is planned as a free app, with no time limit. The public release is in preparation. All five sizes, both densities, all three palettes, and every setting are included. Available widget sizes depend on your macOS version.

What changes if I pay?

You get the same full app. Paying any amount is an optional, one-time way to support BatteryGrid’s independent development. There is no subscription and no extra feature unlock. Optional payments are coming soon; the public download is not available yet.

Which devices can I see?

Your Mac, compatible keyboards, mice, trackpads, audio accessories, and other sources that provide battery data. Support depends on the model and the data available in macOS. An icon in the catalog does not by itself mean a device is automatically supported.

What about AirPods and the case?

BatteryGrid shows the AirPods data available to it: the earbuds, and separate left, right, and case readings when provided. macOS does not always expose every value or charging status, and some readings may be cached. The app does not invent missing percentages.

Does it replace the system widget?

BatteryGrid is a separate app with its own widgets. Add a size from the system gallery. You can keep Apple Batteries alongside it.

Why are updates sometimes delayed?

The app reads available sources in the background, while macOS decides when widgets refresh. A widget therefore cannot guarantee an update every second.

How were these comparisons made?

The Apple images shown here are actual screenshots supplied by the app’s owner on October 16, 2026: a full-color widget gallery and a tinted medium desktop widget. The original files are unchanged. The comparison concerns the version and controls observed on our test Mac. The medium capture shows four devices, a faint charging bolt, and an order that is not lowest charge first.

The opening comparison pairs an actual Apple gallery capture with the native Polar render used in the palette preview: ten distinct icons, sorted lowest charge first, with two free slots at regular density. BatteryGrid’s comparison examples are native SwiftUI renders with illustrative readings, not readings taken from the Apple screenshots.

The comparison has two modes. Regular shows a four-cell BatteryGrid example sorted 10%, 44%, 75%, 100%, alongside the unchanged Apple medium capture. Compact retains the five-cell native example in the same footprint. Both modes call out the faint charging cue in the tinted Apple screenshot, the separate BatteryGrid charging badge, and custom icon availability. Pinned cells are an optional BatteryGrid setting; neither preview has pins applied. Icon choice changes appearance, not automatic battery support.

The palette previews use lowest-charge-first sorting with no pins. Each palette has ten distinct icons, leaving two regular cells or five compact cells free, and a Regular/Compact control. The sizes section shows all ten native size and density renders at the same scale, with illustrative readings. Select any preview to view it full size. In the app, selection, pins, sorting, and colors apply across BatteryGrid widgets on the same Mac.
